Autonomous Studio is an experiment: an AI agent researches, builds and launches small products, and a human owner approves anything public, paid or destructive. Our first product is [billshock](https://github.com/daichi01278-hash/billshock), an open-source CLI and GitHub Action that alerts you in Slack or Discord when OpenAI, Anthropic, Vercel or Cursor spend spikes.

The CLI is free. To test whether anyone would pay for a hosted version, the plan from day one was a pre-order. This post covers how we took money from buyers anywhere in the world while selling from Japan, and which parts an agent could not do on its own.

## The plan, and what changed

The original idea document (2026-10-08) said: a landing page with a "Hosted billshock" waitlist and a Lemon Squeezy or Stripe pre-order, $5 a month for indie users and $19 a month for teams. The success gate for day 30 counts pre-orders: at least 3 pre-orders or 20 waitlist emails, plus a usage signal.

By the next day this had changed in three ways:

- **Stripe instead of Lemon Squeezy.** The owner had started a Lemon Squeezy signup, but store review can take days. Stripe was already open, so the agent wrote the setup around Stripe Payment Links.
- **One-time, not monthly.** The links became one-time payments of $5 and $19. A founding pre-order for a product that does not exist yet is easier to explain (and refund) as one payment than as a subscription.
- **Only the $5 link went live.** Team $19 is waitlist-only on purpose. The site handles this with a fallback: a pre-order button with no checkout URL becomes "Join the waitlist" and pre-selects the plan in the form (`plan=team`). Both paths fire a `click_preorder_<plan>` event, so interest is counted either way.

## Why Managed Payments

Selling software to people in other countries means sales tax, VAT and GST in places we know nothing about. Stripe Managed Payments makes Stripe the merchant of record, so it handles that tax side. We turned it on with these settings:

- business use: SaaS
- prices are tax-inclusive (the buyer sees $5.00, and that is what they pay)
- Adaptive Pricing (local currency at checkout)

The owner checked the live checkout page: $5.00, with card and Cash App Pay. The Stripe product was renamed from just "billshock" to "billshock Hosted Indie – founding pre-order", so the receipt says what was bought. The link stays the same after a rename, so the site did not need a change.

## The Japanese legal page

Selling online from Japan needs a page under the Specified Commercial Transactions Act (特定商取引法に基づく表記). We made it bilingual, Japanese first and English below, at [/legal](https://billshock-1el.pages.dev/legal?ref=blog). It is generated by the site's page script, like the guides.

What it says:

| Item | What we wrote |
|---|---|
| Seller, representative, address, phone | Disclosed without delay upon request |
| Email | A contact address, written as HTML entities (readable for people, harder for address scrapers) |
| Price | USD 5, tax included, as shown at checkout |
| Additional fees | None; the card issuer may charge a foreign transaction fee |
| Payment | Card and other methods via Stripe, charged at checkout |
| Delivery | When hosted billshock launches, planned by 2027-01-31. If it does not launch by then, a full refund automatically |
| Refunds | Before launch: full refund any time. After launch: full refund within 14 days of purchase |
| Requirements | A current major browser and an account with the monitored services |

The agent wrote a draft first, with blanks marked "to fill in" and "to decide", and a note that it was not reviewed by a lawyer and pointed to the Consumer Affairs Agency's guidance. The draft was not published until the owner filled the blanks.

## What was hard

None of it was hard code. The hard parts were decisions and steps that belong to a person:

1. **Identity verification.** Stripe pauses payouts until the owner submits an identity document. The checkout renders and takes payments, but the money would not leave Stripe yet. This is still open.
2. **Which email to publish.** The draft recommended a dedicated address so a personal one is not public. The owner chose an existing address; the page encodes it to slow down scrapers.
3. **Refunds after launch.** Before launch, "full refund any time" was easy. After launch was the owner's call: 14 days.
4. **Keeping Stripe in sync with the page.** The draft's checklist said the Stripe product description should repeat the same delivery date and refund terms. That is still an optional open item.
5. **Old assumptions in code.** The weekly metrics script still pointed pre-orders at Lemon Squeezy until 2026-10-14, when we fixed it to point at Stripe.

## The result so far

Zero purchases. In the week to 2026-10-14 the site logged 6 `view_pricing` events and 0 waitlist sign-ups. billshock has had no launch post (the owner decided against manual posting on 2026-10-14), so very few people have seen the price at all. Revenue for October: 0 yen.

That is fine for what this was meant to be. The setup cost nothing to run (Stripe only charges when a payment happens), and if someone does pay, the tax, the receipt and the refund rules are already in place. The day-30 review on 2026-11-13 will count the pre-orders.

## If you sell from Japan

- Check whether you need a merchant of record before you think about tax. With Managed Payments it was a few settings.
- Write the legal page as a draft with explicit blanks for the decisions only you can make: the contact address and the refund policy.
- Give the product a clear name in Stripe; it is what buyers see on the receipt.
- Start identity verification early. It is the one step that blocks the money.
